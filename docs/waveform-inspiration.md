### 1.5 Apple Human Interface Guidelines on audio, haptics, and motion

Use system sound services for short sounds and vibrations (Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-audio). Respect silent switch behavior, where only explicitly initiated media continues, and never redefine audio controls to mean something new (Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-audio). For custom UI elements, design custom sounds that aid location and feedback, and vary pitch plus volume slightly on repeated sounds so frequent triggers stay pleasant (Sources: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-audio and https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10271/). Match sound timing to transitions and keep frequent UI sounds subtle (Source: https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10271/).

Pair sound with haptics where hardware allows. Use system haptic patterns per documented meaning, keep one causal link between a pattern and its action, prefer short haptics for discrete events, match intensity and sharpness to the animation, synchronize sound with haptics, and always offer a mute path (Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics). The WWDC audio haptics talk adds three working rules called harmony, utility, and moderation. Design sound, haptics, and visuals together, add feedback only where it communicates value, and prototype by removing what feels gratuitous (Source: https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2019/810/). Sharp transients pair with chime attacks while smooth ramps pair with continuous swells, though the talk encourages testing the opposite when it feels better (Source: https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2019/810/).

Motion rules are strict. Add motion purposefully, never as decoration, and never as the only channel for important information (Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion). Favor realistic feedback that follows gestures, avoid extra motion on frequently repeated interactions, and let people cancel motion instead of waiting on it (Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion). Avoid sustained oscillation, especially near 0.2 Hz, keep amplitude low and content translucent if oscillation is required (Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion).

Accessibility rules extend motion and audio. Provide feedback through color plus text plus sound plus haptics so silenced, glance away, and VoiceOver cases all still work (Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/feedback). Pair audio cues with matching haptics for people who cannot perceive audio or have it off (Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility). When Reduce Motion is active, cut automatic and repetitive animation including zoom, scale, and peripheral motion, and prefer replacements such as tighter springs, gesture tracked motion, axis fades instead of slides, and no blur animation (Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility). App Store review guidance confirms the same triggers. Disable or change depth simulation, multi axis motion, spinning, vortex effects, and auto advancing motion when the user prefers reduced motion, and use dissolves or color shifts where motion carried meaning (Source: https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/reduced-motion-evaluation-criteria).

Speech pipeline note. Apple current speech API separates volatile preliminary results from final results, with a progressive long dictation preset that refines text as more audio arrives (Source: https://developer.apple.com/documentation/speech/recognizing-speech-in-live-audio). The WWDC talk behind it describes the same split between fast volatile transcripts and finalized transcripts that replace them (Source: https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/277/). This supports showing live partials early and settling them later, which matches our chunked local STT better than a paste on release model.

## 3. WebAudio sound cue craft for desktop overlays

### 3.1 Spec primitives

The Web Audio spec centers on modular routing of AudioNodes, with OscillatorNode for periodic tones, GainNode for explicit gain, PeriodicWave for custom timbres, and AudioParam automation for envelopes, fades, sweeps, and LFO shapes (Source: https://webaudio.github.io/web-audio-api/). The W3C published Recommendation carries the same node set and factory methods such as createOscillator and createGain (Source: https://www.w3.org/TR/webaudio-1.1/).

MDN documents the working parts. OscillatorNode produces a constant tone with frequency in hertz and detune in cents, defaulting to 440 Hz middle A (Source: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/OscillatorNode). GainNode multiplies samples by a unitless gain, and direct value changes cause clicks, so callers must use AudioParam interpolation methods instead (Source: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/GainNode). AudioParam supports setValueAtTime, linearRampToValueAtTime, exponentialRampToValueAtTime, setTargetAtTime, and setValueCurveAtTime, all scheduled against AudioContext currentTime (Source: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AudioParam).

Method specifics matter for click free earcons. Linear ramps move gain linearly to a target at an end time (Source: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AudioParam/linearRampToValueAtTime). Exponential ramps follow an exponential curve and suit frequency changes because hearing is logarithmic, but the target must stay positive, so fades use 0.01 or 0.0001 instead of zero (Sources: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AudioParam/exponentialRampToValueAtTime and https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/OscillatorNode). Value curves step through an array of floats spread over a duration with linear interpolation between points (Source: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AudioParam/setValueCurveAtTime). The MDN audio param demo wires all of these to buttons with two second examples, which reads directly as recipe source (Sources: https://mdn.github.io/webaudio-examples/audio-param/ and https://github.com/mdn/webaudio-examples/blob/main/audio-param/index.html).

### 3.2 Duration, frequency, and envelope guidance

No W3C or Apple page standardizes earcon hertz or milliseconds. Apple guidance stays qualitative. Keep UI sounds subtle, short, and matched to transition timing, and randomize pitch and loudness slightly when a sound repeats often (Sources: https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10271/ and https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-audio). The audio haptics talk adds that confirmation sounds should read as short, precise, and clear rather than serious or heavy (Source: https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2019/810/).

The numbers below are craft practice synthesized from those constraints plus MDN envelope mechanics, not quoted standards. Keep every cue under about 250 ms. Use sine or triangle waves to stay soft. Start each cue from silence with setValueAtTime zero. Shape loudness with a 5 to 15 ms attack and a 60 to 150 ms decay. Avoid square and sawtooth for frequent cues because their harmonics fatigue fast.

Zero asset recipe pattern, OscillatorNode into GainNode into destination.

```js
function blip({ freq = 880, endFreq = null, dur = 0.12, attack = 0.008, peak = 0.15, type = "sine" }) {
  const t = ctx.currentTime;
  const osc = ctx.createOscillator();
  const g = ctx.createGain();
  osc.type = type;
  osc.frequency.setValueAtTime(freq, t);
  if (endFreq) osc.frequency.exponentialRampToValueAtTime(endFreq, t + dur);
  g.gain.setValueAtTime(0.0, t);
  g.gain.linearRampToValueAtTime(peak, t + attack);
  g.gain.exponentialRampToValueAtTime(0.0001, t + dur);
  osc.connect(g).connect(ctx.destination);
  osc.start(t);
  osc.stop(t + dur + 0.02);
}
```

Suggested starting points for user testing, all values provisional.

Start and listening cue. Sine 880 Hz, 120 ms, attack 8 ms, peak gain 0.12 to 0.18. Slight upward glide to 990 Hz signals readiness without a harsh edge.

Stop cue. Triangle 660 Hz, 90 ms, attack 5 ms, peak gain 0.10 to 0.15. Downward glide to 520 Hz reads as closure.

Done and injected cue. Two sine pings, 660 Hz then 990 Hz, 90 ms each with 40 ms gap, peak gain 0.12. The pair mirrors Apple double tap confirmation logic from the haptics talk without copying any Apple file (Source: https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2019/810/).

Error cue. Sine 220 Hz with gentle glide to 180 Hz, 180 ms, peak gain 0.12. One low cue only. Never loop it. Pair it with coral border plus text so muted users get the same signal, per the multimodal feedback rule (Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/feedback).

Desktop integration notes. Create the AudioContext lazily and resume it inside the user gesture that starts dictation, since autoplay policy suspends contexts without gestures. Expose a sound toggle in settings that defaults on but persists off, mirroring the Wispr desktop Sound Effects path (Source: https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/6409258247-starting-your-first-dictation). Keep cue gain independent of mic gain. Randomize playback pitch by plus or minus 2 percent and gain by plus or minus 1 dB on repeats to reduce fatigue, per Apple randomization guidance (Source: https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10271/).

## 4. Reactive waveform rendering architectures

### 4.1 AnalyserNode plus canvas baseline

AnalyserNode passes audio through unchanged while exposing real time frequency and time domain data (Source: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AnalyserNode). Key controls are fftSize, frequencyBinCount at half fftSize, minDecibels and maxDecibels scaling, and smoothingTimeConstant for frame to frame averaging (Source: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AnalyserNode). Data lands in caller provided Float32Array or Uint8Array through getFloatTimeDomainData, getByteTimeDomainData, getFloatFrequencyData, and getByteFrequencyData (Source: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AnalyserNode).

MDN canonical visualization recipe uses fftSize 2048, one Uint8 array sized to frequencyBinCount, a requestAnimationFrame loop that refreshes time domain data each frame, and a single stroked path across canvas width (Source: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Web_Audio_API/Visualizations_with_Web_Audio_API). That page also points to the Voice change O matic demo for complete applied code (Source: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Web_Audio_API/Visualizations_with_Web_Audio_API). For a pill meter this full oscilloscope is oversampled. Read a small slice or compute peak and RMS per frame instead of stroking 1024 points.

### 4.2 Ring buffer peak metering with attack and release

Production meters separate instant peaks from smoothed display values. The Meter Madness component docs expose attack time default 10 ms, release time default 100 ms, peak hold 1000 ms, clip at minus 3 dB, warn at minus 12 dB, and floor at minus 48 dB (Source: https://www.magic.mov/docs/spells/meter-madness). It measures RMS, peak, and average from AnalyserNode sources with channel splitting for stereo (Source: https://www.magic.mov/docs/spells/meter-madness).

Elapsed time ballistics beat frame count ballistics. The waveform component library processes spectrum frames with attackMs, releaseMs, and inertiaMs applied against timestamps, so behavior holds across 60 Hz and 120 Hz displays, and its fastPeaks mode bypasses attack only for rising peaks while RMS keeps configured smoothing (Source: https://github.com/gvastethecreator/waveform-component). Copy that split. Let rising edges react in about 10 to 35 ms and let falls relax over 100 to 240 ms. Keep a short peak hold near one second so transients stay visible without freezing the meter.

Susurro already gates display level through a square root curve on trimmed peaks rather than raw peaks, with startup transient skip and VAD gating. That matches the smoothing intent above. Keep that gate and add the attack release follower in front of the bar renderer.

### 4.3 Idle and breathing animation when silent

When VAD reports silence, stop tracking the mic and run a low amplitude drift. A slow opacity or scale cycle around two to four seconds reads as alive without implying input. Gate it behind motion preference. Detect matchMedia prefers reduce motion, freeze the drift when reduce matches, and show a static dot or a gentle opacity dissolve instead of movement (Sources: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/At-rules/@media/prefers-reduced-motion and https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Techniques/client-side-script/SCR40). Web.dev shows the same listener pattern for stopping JavaScript animation on preference change without reload (Source: https://web.dev/articles/prefers-reduced-motion). This directly implements Apple rules to make motion optional and to replace motion with fades where motion carried meaning (Sources: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion and https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility).

Never use rotation, spin, sustained bounce, or parallax in the pill. Those are the exact vestibular triggers Apple and review guidance call out (Sources: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion and https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/reduced-motion-evaluation-criteria).

### 4.4 Morphing waveform into progress

Two proven techniques cover the listening to transcribing morph without layout thrash.

Layered canvas progress from wavesurfer.js. The renderer draws the wave once to a canvas, clones it to a progress canvas, sets globalCompositeOperation to source in, and fills the progress layer in the progress color as a mask (Source: https://github.com/katspaugh/wavesurfer.js/blob/main/src/renderer.ts). Played versus unplayed split then becomes a width clip, not a redraw. The core docs define waveColor for unplayed and progressColor for played regions (Source: https://wavesurfer.xyz/docs/core-concepts/). For Susurro, map waveColor to a neutral light bar and progressColor to teal fill that sweeps as transcription plus injection advance. Precompute peaks for long audio rather than decoding twice, since the project warns large files decoded through both media and WebAudio paths cause memory pressure (Source: https://wavesurfer.xyz/docs/core-concepts/). Our pill is live mic rather than file playback, so the direct lesson is to keep one peak buffer and reuse it for both live bars and the progress sweep.

Shared element transitions from the View Transition API. Tagging an element with view transition name captures old and new snapshots as pseudo elements and animates size plus position between DOM states, with old and new captures coexisting in an overlay layer (Sources: https://www.w3.org/TR/css-view-transitions-1/ and https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/View_Transition_API/Using). Chrome docs describe the same snapshot, DOM update under suppressed rendering, then CSS animation sequence for same and cross document cases (Source: https://developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/view-transitions). The API is enhancement only. The DOM update still applies when the transition cannot run, including duplicate name cases (Sources: https://www.w3.org/TR/css-view-transitions-1/ and https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/ViewTransition). For a 364x64 pill the practical move is narrower than full view transitions. Keep one canvas element across states and animate a progress clip plus a crossfade between bar mode and determinate fill. Reserve true shared element transitions for a future expand to history detail view, not for the per frame meter.

### 4.5 Holding 60fps without layout thrash

Fix the canvas once. Size to 364x64 CSS pixels times devicePixelRatio, cache the width, and never read layout inside the frame loop. Preallocate one Float32Array plus one byte array outside the loop and reuse them to avoid garbage collection spikes. This follows the zero allocation pattern used by real time analyzer builds, though our scale is far smaller.

Draw cheap. Batch the bars into one beginPath with fillRect calls or one stroked path, avoid per frame state churn, avoid shadowBlur, avoid per frame text, and prefer clearRect over canvas resize for clearing (Source: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Canvas_API/Tutorial/Optimizing_canvas). Drive frames with requestAnimationFrame, which fires before repaint and pauses in background tabs, instead of setInterval (Sources: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/window/requestAnimationFrame and https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Canvas_API/Tutorial/Optimizing_canvas). Use the callback timestamp delta for smoothing math so 60 Hz, 120 Hz, and 144 Hz displays behave alike, since refresh rates vary (Source: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/window/requestAnimationFrame). Keep DOM motion to transform and opacity so the compositor handles it off the main thread (Source: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Performance/Guides/CSS_JavaScript_animation_performance). Render only on new audio or state change. When silent and reduced motion is off, a 2 Hz opacity breath at low amplitude is enough. When reduced motion is on, stop the loop or render one static frame.

Open source file pointers for implementers. Waveform renderer with bar versus line paths and multi canvas lazy rendering (Source: https://github.com/katspaugh/wavesurfer.js/blob/main/src/renderer.ts). Peak export with max pooling over sample windows (Source: https://github.com/katspaugh/wavesurfer.js/blob/main/src/wavesurfer.ts). Project overview and plugin set including the Record plugin that renders mic input waveforms (Source: https://github.com/katspaugh/wavesurfer.js/). MDN oscilloscope plus spectrum bar recipes (Source: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Web_Audio_API/Visualizations_with_Web_Audio_API). Meter ballistics and stereo metering component API (Source: https://www.magic.mov/docs/spells/meter-madness). Timestamp based attack release plus fast peak dynamics processor (Source: https://github.com/gvastethecreator/waveform-component).

## 5. Steal list for the 364x64 pill

Constraints. One fixed frame, bottom center dock hint, two accents only. Teal signals live levels and forward progress. Coral signals errors only. Everything else stays neutral.

1. One frame, three contents, no resize. Keep Idle, Listening, and Transcribing inside the same 364x64 canvas. Crossfade content and sweep a teal progress clip instead of growing the pill. This follows Apple concentric fit and two second animation cap without copying the Island (Sources: https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10194/ and https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/live-activities).
2. Five to seven gated bars, not a full oscilloscope. Drive bar height from VAD gated peak through square root scaling with 10 ms attack and 100 to 240 ms release, plus one second peak hold. Freeze or collapse to baseline when VAD says silence. Ballistic defaults come from meter practice (Source: https://www.magic.mov/docs/spells/meter-madness) with timestamp based smoothing so high refresh displays match (Source: https://github.com/gvastethecreator/waveform-component).
3. Synthesized ping pair with a real mute switch. Start cue sine 880 Hz for 120 ms, stop cue triangle 660 Hz for 90 ms, done cue two step 660 then 990 Hz, error cue single low 220 to 180 Hz sine. Build each from OscillatorNode plus GainNode with scheduled ramps and no audio files (Sources: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/OscillatorNode, https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/GainNode, and https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AudioParam). Put the toggle in settings, default on, persist off, per the Wispr desktop path (Source: https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/6409258247-starting-your-first-dictation).
4. Breathing idle that respects reduced motion. Slow opacity drift at about 0.25 to 0.5 Hz when idle, static dot plus text when prefers reduce motion matches. Use the standard media query plus change listener pattern (Sources: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/At-rules/@media/prefers-reduced-motion, https://web.dev/articles/prefers-reduced-motion, and https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Techniques/client-side-script/SCR40). This also satisfies Apple rules on optional motion and fade replacement (Sources: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion and https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility).
5. Processing morph with cancel preserved. Collapse bars into a left to right teal determinate fill as STT plus cleanup plus injection advance, with a low amplitude shimmer while duration is unknown. Keep Esc and cancel visible through processing, since Wispr keeps only Cancel during processing (Source: https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/6391241694-use-flow-hands-free). Never block a new session silently. Show the blocked reason with retry, mirroring Flow still processing behavior (Source: https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/6409258247-starting-your-first-dictation).
6. Error treatment in coral plus words, never sound alone. Coral border plus short sentence case message plus Retry affordance. One soft low cue maximum. This follows multimodal feedback rules (Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/feedback) and avoids Wispr Android style non mutable sound (Source: https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/6409258247-starting-your-first-dictation).
7. Dock manners from Wispr pain. Default bottom center, remember user edge, never cover the focused control, pass through clicks on transparent regions, and hide rather than fight fullscreen or capture modes. Wispr learned docking the hard way after a fixed bottom bar covered send buttons and the Dock (Sources: https://wisprflow.ai/whats-new and https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/1790396454-move-and-dock-the-flow-bar-on-desktop).

## 6. Non goals

Full screen edge glow. Apple edge light works because the OS owns the whole display compositor. Our pill is one 364x64 client window and Wayland owns placement. A glow that escapes our window is impossible without compositor effects, and faking it with blur and shadow inside 64 px height wastes our tiny budget. Keep glow inside the frame as a teal progress edge.

Multi activity Island juggling. Compact, expanded, and minimal views solve phone multitasking across timers, calls, scores, and rides. Our pill tracks one dictation session. Do not build stacked or sidecar states.

Multicolor orb gradients. Siri glow blends many hues. Our palette allows two accents. Teal means working, coral means failed. A rainbow meter would break contrast discipline and confuse error signaling.

Paste on release only. Wispr holds audio, transcribes full context on release, then pastes polished text (Source: https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/2772472373-what-is-flow). That latency suits cloud cleanup. Susurro is offline first with chunked local STT and must show volatile partials early like Apple progressive dictation (Source: https://developer.apple.com/documentation/speech/recognizing-speech-in-live-audio). Do not hide all feedback until release.

Always on bubble with snooze and shake. Wispr mobile bubble needs drag, snooze, shake to restore, and per field visibility rules because it floats over other apps (Sources: https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/2772472373-what-is-flow and https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/6391241694-use-flow-hands-free). Our Linux pill placement is compositor owned through a Hyprland rule hint. Do not rebuild bubble window management. Offer show, hide, and quiet hours instead.

Frequent or long sounds. Apple warns that frequent motion plus sound tires users and that UI sounds must stay subtle (Sources: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion and https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10271/). No per chunk ticks, no looping processing tone, no sound on every bar peak.

Spinners, rotation, and parallax. These trigger vestibular discomfort and violate Apple motion and review guidance (Sources: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion and https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/reduced-motion-evaluation-criteria). Use determinate fill plus opacity shimmer, never rotation.

Dictation into secrets. Wispr hides its bubble in password, PIN, numeric, phone, and banking fields (Sources: https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/2296441257-starting-your-first-dictation and https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/5090227041-dictating-in-notification-reply-boxes-and-browser-address-bars). Copy the hiding. Never offer to dictate into a secrets field.

Cloud style history sync and 14 day audio vault. Wispr retry depends on stored device audio with expiry and retention modes (Source: https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/2503460374-retry-failed-transcriptions). Our history lives in local SQLite with best effort upserts. Keep retry scoped to the current session plus short local history. Do not promise cross device transcripts.

Double decode playback architectures. Wavesurfer warns that WebAudio backend playback plus separate waveform decode fetches and decodes twice and strains memory on long files (Source: https://wavesurfer.xyz/docs/core-concepts/). Our pill meters live mic input. Never decode a recording just to draw levels. Meter the capture tap directly.
